Ten Principles for Good Design

Dieter Rams' famous design philosophy, in my own words, with one image per principle showing what it means to me.

01. Good design is innovative

New technology keeps opening doors for new design solutions, but innovation should never be chased just for its own sake. It has to move forward alongside real advances, not just for the sake of looking different.

02. Good design is useful

A product exists to be used, so it has to work well. But "useful" isn't only functional. It should also feel right and look right, since people respond to a product emotionally as much as practically.

03. Good design is aesthetic

How something looks isn't separate from how well it works. The things we use every day shape our mood and wellbeing, so beauty is part of the function, not decoration on top of it.

04. Good design is understandable

A well-designed object explains itself. You shouldn't need a manual to figure out what something does or how to use it. The design itself should make the logic obvious.

05. Good design is unobtrusive

Products aren't meant to be decoration or a personal statement, they're tools. Good design steps back and leaves room for the person using it, instead of demanding attention.

06. Good design is honest

It doesn't pretend a product is more innovative, powerful, or valuable than it actually is, and it doesn't try to manipulate someone with promises the product can't keep.

07. Good design is long-lasting

It avoids being trendy, so it doesn't look dated after a year or two. Good design lasts for years, even in a throwaway culture that's constantly moving on.

08. Good design is thorough down to the last detail

Nothing about a product should feel arbitrary or left to chance. Care and precision in every detail is a sign of respect for the person who'll actually use it.

09. Good design is environmentally friendly

It conserves resources and minimizes physical and visual pollution throughout a product's whole lifecycle, from how it's made, to how it's used, to how it's eventually thrown away.

10. Good design is as little design as possible

Less, but better. Concentrate on the essential features and leave out anything that doesn't need to be there. Simplicity, done well, is its own kind of purity.
